PlanSwift vs Bluebeam for takeoffs

Bluebeam Revu is PDF markup, measurement and document collaboration software that many estimators also use for takeoff. Its plans run $260–$590 per user per year (Bluebeam pricing). PlanSwift is takeoff and estimating software with pre-built trade assemblies. It costs $2,000/year for one Essential seat or $3,000/year for one Core seat, with discounts for more seats (PlanSwift pricing). If you need takeoff plus a priced estimate in one tool, PlanSwift is built for that. If your team lives in PDFs for markups, RFIs and document control and does takeoff on the side, Bluebeam usually wins. Neither one chases sub quotes.

What each does well

Bluebeam Revu

  • PDF markup, redaction, stamps and forms. It's the everyday document tool for a lot of GCs and design teams.
  • Measurements set to drawing scale: length and area on all plans, plus perimeter, count, volume and angle on Core and up.
  • Overlay and compare drawing revisions (Core and up). Max adds AI "Smart Overlay" comparisons and "Smart Review" checks for things like missing sheets and fixture tags.
  • Studio real-time collaboration on the same files, on web and mobile too.
  • Integrations listed with Procore, Autodesk Forma, SharePoint, OneDrive, Box, Dropbox and others.

(All from bluebeam.com/pricing, Oct 16, 2026.)

PlanSwift

  • Takeoff and estimating in one tool, with pre-built assemblies for various trades (PlanSwift Essential plan page).
  • AI tools on Essential: Auto Count, Auto Scale, Auto Bookmark. Core adds the Takeoff BOOST suite and Auto Takeoff, which generates takeoff drafts (PlanSwift pricing).
  • Essential includes priority support and 2 hours of live 1-on-1 training (Essential plan page).
  • Known for its drag-and-drop assembly library and Excel integration. It's part of ConstructConnect (ConstructConnect blog, read Oct 16, 2026; vendor source).

Limits

Bluebeam

  • It's a PDF tool first. Quantities go to Excel with Quantity Link (Complete and up), and pricing usually happens there or in another estimating program.
  • Revu desktop runs on Windows. Mac users get web and mobile (Bluebeam FAQ).
  • Owners of perpetual Revu 20 licenses: Bluebeam says Revu 20 reaches end of support July 31, 2026 and end of life December 31, 2026 (Bluebeam FAQ). If that's you, you're choosing a subscription either way.
  • The Max price is introductory. Later renewals will be "at standard list price, announced at a future time."

PlanSwift

  • Windows-based (ConstructConnect).
  • Annual plans only. The page says no ownership option ("Essential and Core are annual plans") (PlanSwift).
  • Higher per-seat cost than Bluebeam.

Which to pick

  • Pick Bluebeam if your team already marks up, compares and shares PDFs all day, your takeoffs are mostly measuring and counting, and you price in Excel. Core or Complete is the realistic tier.
  • Pick PlanSwift if you're a trade contractor or estimator who wants assemblies and pricing tied to the takeoff, and you're fine on Windows.
  • Some teams run both: Bluebeam for documents and RFIs, PlanSwift for estimating. Price both seats before you assume that's cheaper than one alternative.

FAQ

Asked on every call.

Is PlanSwift or Bluebeam cheaper? +
As of Oct 16, 2026, Bluebeam's published plans run $260 to $590 per user per year, while PlanSwift lists $2,000 per year for one Essential seat and $3,000 for one Core seat, with discounts for more seats. Bluebeam is cheaper per seat, but PlanSwift includes estimating features Bluebeam does not.
Can Bluebeam do estimating? +
Bluebeam Revu measures and counts on PDFs and, on the Complete plan and up, links quantities to Excel with Quantity Link. Pricing the estimate typically happens in Excel or another estimating tool.
Do PlanSwift and Bluebeam run on Mac? +
Bluebeam Revu runs on Windows, with web and mobile access included in all plans. ConstructConnect describes PlanSwift as Windows-based.
