# Subcontractor compliance software vs a spreadsheet tracker

*Checked October 16, 2026 (ET). BuildWithBNZ publishes this page and is one of the options listed. This is general information for US contractors, not legal or insurance advice. Your subcontract, your owner contract and your insurance broker set the real requirements.*

**The short answer:** Subcontractor compliance software collects sub paperwork, tracks expiration dates and sends reminders for you. A spreadsheet tracker does the same job by hand. A spreadsheet works fine when you have a few subs and one person who checks it every week. Software starts to pay off when you have many subs, many jobs, or nobody with time to chase renewals.

## What "sub compliance" means

A few terms, in plain words:

- A **COI** (certificate of insurance) is a short summary from the sub's insurance agent. It lists the sub's policies, limits and policy dates.
- An **expiration date** is when a policy ends. Each policy can renew on a different date.
- **Additional insured** means your company is covered under the sub's policy. A certificate alone may not prove it, so ask your broker what proof you need.
- A **W-9** is the IRS form a sub fills out to give you its taxpayer ID number. The IRS says it's used to give a correct TIN to payers who must file information returns ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-9)).

Compliance tracking means you know, for every sub, which papers you have, which ones are expiring, and who's chasing the rest.

Check your insurance and contract requirements with your insurance agent or lawyer.

## The spreadsheet tracker

Most GCs start with a spreadsheet in Excel or Google Sheets. A good one has:

| Column | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Sub name and trade | Who you're tracking |
| Contact and broker email | Who to ask for renewals |
| One expiration date per policy | Policies renew on different dates |
| W-9 on file (yes/no) | You need it before you pay |
| Last checked | Shows whether anyone is looking |
| Owner | Who chases the gaps |
| Status | Compliant, expiring, expired or missing |

Sort by the earliest expiration date. That's the row that will bite you first.

**Where spreadsheets work:**
- You have a short list of active subs.
- One person owns the sheet and checks it on a set day each week.
- Your jobs are short, so few policies expire mid-job.

**Where spreadsheets break:**
- Nobody owns it, so it goes stale.
- Several people edit it, and dates get overwritten.
- Certificates live in email and folders, not linked to the row.
- Reminders depend on someone remembering to look.

## What subcontractor compliance software adds

Most compliance tools promise the same core jobs:

1. **Collection.** They request documents from subs and store them in one place.
2. **Tracking.** They read or record dates and flag what's expiring or missing.
3. **Reminders.** They follow up with the sub or broker before a policy lapses.
4. **A status view.** You see who's compliant at a glance.

Two examples:

- **TrustLayer** describes itself as a COI tracker for risk professionals ([trustlayer.io](https://trustlayer.io/)).
- **Billy** describes itself as COI tracking and compliance software for construction ([billyforinsurance.com](https://www.billyforinsurance.com/)).

Neither lists a price we can quote here. Ask each vendor for a quote, and ask what's included at your number of subs.

**Questions to ask any vendor:**
- Does it read the certificate for you, or does someone type the dates in?
- Who does the follow-up: the software, the vendor's team, or you?
- Does it check additional insured proof, or only the certificate?
- Do subs need to create an account to upload?
- Can it export the data if you leave?

## Spreadsheet or software: how to choose

| If… | Lean toward |
|---|---|
| You have few subs and one owner who checks weekly | A spreadsheet tracker |
| You run many jobs at once with lots of subs | Compliance software |
| Missed renewals have already caused a problem | Compliance software, or a named owner for the sheet |
| You also need help chasing quotes and bid paperwork | A tool that covers the sub side, not just insurance |

Whichever you pick, give it one owner. A tracker with no owner fails in software just like it fails in a spreadsheet.

## FAQ

**What is subcontractor compliance software?**
Software that collects and tracks sub paperwork, such as COIs and W-9s. It flags what's missing or expiring and sends reminders.

**Is a spreadsheet good enough to track sub compliance?**
Often, for a small company, if one person owns it and checks dates every week.

**What should a subcontractor compliance tracker include?**
One row per sub, one column per policy expiration date, W-9 status, last-checked date, an owner and a status.

**What does a W-9 do?**
Per the IRS, it gives a correct taxpayer ID number to a payer that must file information returns.
