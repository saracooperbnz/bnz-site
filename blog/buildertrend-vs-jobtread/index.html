Buildertrend vs JobTread for remodelers and home builders

Checked October 17, 2026 (ET), on each vendor's own site. BuildWithBNZ publishes this page and doesn't sell either tool.

The short answer: Buildertrend vs JobTread comes down to the size of your shop and how you like to buy. Both are built for remodelers and home builders. Both run your schedule, client updates, change orders and job costs in one place. Buildertrend aims at "more established builders" and gives each company a custom quote. JobTread posts one price with every feature included. If you run a remodeling business, demo both on a real job before you decide.

A few terms first:

  • A change order is a written change to the job's scope and price, signed by the client.
  • Selections are the finishes and fixtures the homeowner picks, like tile or faucets.
  • A client portal is a login where homeowners see progress, approve changes and pay.
  • A sub portal is a login where your subs see schedules and documents.

Buildertrend vs JobTread: what remodelers should compare

1. Who each one is built for

Buildertrend says it was "built for builders who" oversee 5+ projects a year or handle complex, multi-phase builds, and want to grow from 6 to 8 figures in revenue (Buildertrend pricing, Oct 17, 2026). It calls itself software "for more established builders." It has a page just for remodelers (Buildertrend remodelers).

JobTread lists home builders, remodelers, specialty contractors and commercial contractors among the trades it serves (JobTread pricing, Oct 17, 2026).

2. Change orders and scope changes

Remodels change mid-job. You open a wall and find something nobody planned for. Buildertrend's remodeler page says it "logs it, prices it and gets the homeowner's sign-off the same day." It lists Change Orders, Selections and Budget tools.

JobTread lists Contracts & eSignatures among its features. Ask both vendors to walk you through a change order from field note to signed approval to invoice.

3. Keeping the homeowner in the loop

Your client may be living in the house while you work. Buildertrend lists a Client Portal and a feature called AI Client Updates. It says this "turns your daily logs into a homeowner-ready update automatically."

JobTread says vendors, subcontractors and customers get an external portal "to view and perform a limited set of activities." It says those users are "free and unlimited."

4. Subs and crews

Buildertrend lists a Sub Portal and a Bids tool to "compare subcontractor bids side by side." JobTread says some field crew who only upload photos, log daily notes, view the schedule or check off tasks can be added as free users.

5. Setup and training

JobTread says it gives "free implementation, training, and support." It doesn't offer a free trial, because it assigns every client a Customer Success Manager. It offers a 30-day money-back guarantee on monthly plans.

Buildertrend lists setup and data migration, live virtual trainings, a learning academy and onsite consulting among its support services. It says most builders can start using it "within a few days."

How the pricing models differ

This is where the two split the most.

  • JobTread: $199 per month plus $20 per month for each internal user, with price breaks after 10 users. "No contract & no setup fees." Save 20% on an annual plan (JobTread, Oct 17, 2026).
  • Buildertrend: custom quote. You answer a few questions, then schedule a call. It says pricing includes unlimited users (Buildertrend, Oct 17, 2026).

Many remodelers search "how much does Buildertrend really cost." The honest answer is that it doesn't publish a number. Get the quote in writing, including the term.

Is Buildertrend worth it?

It can be, if you'll use most of what's in it. Ask yourself:

  • Do you run 5 or more projects a year? That's who Buildertrend says it's built for.
  • Will you replace other tools with it, like a separate scheduler, invoicing app or client texting?
  • Will your crew and subs actually log in?

If you answer no to most of these, a lighter tool may fit better.

What about Contractor Foreman?

Some remodelers also compare Buildertrend vs Contractor Foreman. Contractor Foreman's pricing page says its plans start at $49 per month, with a 30-day free period (Contractor Foreman, Oct 17, 2026). Compare its feature list against your must-haves before you switch.

How to choose

  1. List your three biggest headaches. Change orders? Client calls? Late invoices?
  2. Demo with a real job. Bring one recent remodel, including a change order.
  3. Ask who sets it up. Find out who loads your cost codes, templates and clients.
  4. Get the price and term in writing.
  5. Check your accounting. Buildertrend lists QuickBooks, Xero and Gusto integrations. Ask JobTread what it connects to.
