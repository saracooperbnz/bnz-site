NYS prevailing wage rates by county: how to look them up

Checked against NYS Department of Labor and NYC Comptroller pages on October 20 and 21, 2026 (ET). BuildWithBNZ publishes this page.

Not legal advice. This is a general how-to for contractors, not legal or payroll advice. The bid documents, the contracting agency and the official sources below control. Ask a New York construction attorney or your payroll provider about your job.

The short answer: NYS prevailing wage rates by county come from the New York State Department of Labor (NYS DOL). Use its free online search: pick the schedule type, the county and the trade. That shows you the wage and supplement rates for that work. For a real bid, use the schedule in the bid documents, which carries the project's PRC number. For New York City public work, use the NYC Comptroller's schedule instead. We quote no rates here on purpose. They change, so always read them from the official source.

Terms to know first

  • Prevailing wage is the minimum pay the law sets for workers on public work and some other covered jobs in New York. It is set by trade and location. Your crew must get at least that much.
  • Supplements are the benefits part of the rate: things like health, pension and paid time off. You owe the wage and the supplements.
  • A wage schedule (NYS DOL calls it a prevailing rate schedule) is the official list of wage and supplement rates for each trade, plus the overtime, holiday and shift rules that go with them.
  • A PRC number (Prevailing Rate Case number) is the tracking number NYS DOL gives a project when the agency requests its wage schedule.
  • Certified payroll is the signed payroll record you submit to the state. It shows each worker's hours, trade, pay rate and supplements, so the state can check you paid the schedule.

Why rates are set county by county

NYS DOL says its prevailing wage schedules are "issued separately for General Construction Projects and Residential Construction Projects on a county-by-county basis." The same trade can have different rates in two counties next to each other. So the job's location matters as much as the trade.

There are two schedule types:

  • General Construction covers projects such as buildings, heavy and highway, tunnel, and water and sewer work.
  • Residential generally covers building, fixing or tearing down one-family and two-family homes, row housing, and rental units meant for people to live in.

NYS DOL warns that some residential rates apply only in narrow cases. If a trade or location isn't on the Residential schedule, you use the General Construction schedule. DOL asks you to call the local Bureau of Public Work office before you use a Residential schedule, to make sure the project qualifies.

How to look up NYS prevailing wage rates by county

Here is the step-by-step using the official NYS DOL Bureau of Public Work tools.

  1. Go to NYS DOL's Prevailing Wage Schedules page at dol.ny.gov/prevailing-wage-schedules. Click Article 8 Public Construction. (Article 9 is for building service contracts, like cleaning and security, not construction.)
  2. Check the year. The page heading shows the schedule period. On Oct 20, 2026 it read "07/01/2026 - 06/30/2027."
  3. Click "Search Article 8 Prevailing Wage Schedules."
  4. Pick the Schedule Type: General Construction or Residential.
  5. Pick the County where the work will be done.
  6. Pick the trade. Choose an occupation from the list (for example Carpenter, then the right sub-type), or type a keyword such as "tunnel."
  7. Read the whole listing. Note the wage rate, the supplement rate, the effective dates above each column, and any overtime codes and notes.

Want the full county book instead? The same Article 8 page has a Generic Prevailing Wage Schedule By County. Pick your county from the list and it opens a PDF. NYS DOL says the county files are smaller and faster to download than the statewide file.

If you have a PRC number, use Previously Requested Wage Schedules on the Prevailing Wage Schedules page. It lets you find a project's schedule by its PRC number.

Generic rates vs. your project's schedule

This is the step people skip. NYS DOL's Article 8 schedules page says the schedules on the site are "provided for informational and annual determination purposes ONLY," and that "an Original Wage Schedule MUST BE REQUESTED from the Bureau of Public Work."

In plain terms:

  • The contracting agency (the town, school district or other public owner) requests the original schedule from NYS DOL. DOL emails it back with a PRC number. The agency puts that schedule in the bid documents.
  • You bid from the schedule in the bid documents. Use the online county search to plan, check a number or budget a job before the package comes out.
  • If the job looks like prevailing wage work and there's no schedule in the package, ask the agency in writing before you price it.

Reading the schedule: what to look for

The introduction pages of the NYS DOL schedule point out several things that change your labor cost:

  • Classification. NYS DOL decides the right classification based on the type of work: heavy and highway, building, sewer and water, tunnel, or residential. The schedule says "it is the responsibility of the public work contractor to use the proper rate." If you aren't sure, call the district office nearest the project. The schedule lists those offices.
  • Supplements. In most cases supplements are paid at straight time for all hours. But some trades require supplements at a premium rate for overtime hours, or on paid holidays even if no one works. The overtime codes and notes on each trade tell you.
  • Overtime. At a minimum, work over eight hours in a day, or more than five days in a workweek, is overtime on a public work project. Each trade's own overtime rules are listed in the schedule.
  • Effective dates. Look at the dates above each column of rates. A rate holds until the next change listed or until the new yearly schedule on July 1.
  • Shift work. If the contract timeline requires shift work, DOL says it will enforce the shift rate whether or not the contract mentions it.
  • Apprentices. The schedule lists allowed ratios of registered apprentices to journeyworkers for each trade.

Rates can change after you look

NYS DOL publishes the yearly schedule each July 1. Its Prevailing Wage Schedules page says error corrections and updates are posted on the first business day of each month, and that "contractors are responsible for paying these updated rates retroactive to July 1." The Article 8 schedules page keeps a table of each month's changes. When we checked, it showed updates posted for 8/01/2026 and 10/01/2026, and none for 9/01/2026.

Before you bid, and again before a long job crosses July 1, check the updates table for your trades and county.

Work inside New York City

For New York City public work, the NYC Comptroller sets the schedule, not the county search. The Comptroller's Wage Schedules page has:

  • the Construction Worker Schedule (current one published 7/1/2026, effective July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027), and
  • a separate Construction Apprentice Schedule.

The Comptroller's schedule says it was made "solely for Workers, Laborers and Mechanics engaged by private contractors on New York City public work projects." It also says:

  • Contractors must pay the wages and supplements in effect when the work is performed.
  • Changes are published on the Comptroller's website. Preliminary schedules come out around June 1 and final schedules around July 1.
  • Only apprentices individually registered in a NYS DOL-registered program your company takes part in can be paid apprentice rates. Others are paid as journey workers.
  • Before award, questions about trade classifications or rates go to the contracting agency running the bid.

The five boroughs also show up as counties (Bronx, Kings, New York, Queens, Richmond) in the NYS DOL search. Which schedule applies depends on who owns the job, so go by the schedule in the bid documents and ask the agency if it's unclear.

After you find the rates

A few NYS DOL rules tie back to the schedule:

  • Registration. As of December 30, 2024, contractors and subcontractors who bid or do construction work on Article 8 public work or covered private projects must register with NYS DOL. The schedule's introduction says you submit your Certificate of Registration with your bid materials.
  • Certified payroll. NYS DOL's FAQ says contractors and subcontractors must submit payroll at least once every 30 days, starting January 1, 2026, through its Certified Payroll portal. You need your FEIN, your NYS contractor registration number, the project's PRC number and, for public improvement projects, a copy of the payment bond. NYC public work uses NYC's own online certified payroll system instead.
  • Records. The schedule says you must keep accurate payroll records for at least six years.

Common lookup mistakes

  • Using the wrong county, such as where your shop is instead of where the job is.
  • Using a Residential rate when the job needs General Construction.
  • Reading the wage and missing the supplement.
  • Pricing from last year's schedule, or missing a monthly update.
  • Using the generic online search instead of the project's own schedule for the final bid.
  • Using the NYS DOL search for NYC public work.

FAQ

Where do I look up NYS prevailing wage rates by county? Use the NYS Department of Labor's Search Article 8 Prevailing Wage Schedules page. Pick General Construction or Residential, pick the county, then pick an occupation or type a keyword. For New York City public work, use the NYC Comptroller's Construction Worker Schedule instead.

Can I bid off the generic county schedule on the DOL website? Use it to learn and plan, not as your final number. NYS DOL says the schedules posted on its site are for information and the annual determination only, and that an original wage schedule must be requested from the Bureau of Public Work. On a public job, the agency's schedule with its PRC number is the one in the bid documents.

What are supplements on a prevailing wage schedule? Supplements are the benefits part of the rate, such as health, pension and paid time off. You owe the hourly wage plus the supplement rate for each classification. NYS DOL says some classifications also require supplements at a premium rate for overtime hours or on paid holidays.

Do NYS prevailing wage rates change during the year? Yes. NYS DOL publishes the annual schedule on July 1. It posts error corrections and updates on its site on the first business day of each month, and it says contractors must pay those updated rates back to July 1. Check the schedule again before you bid and while you work.

What is a PRC number? PRC stands for Prevailing Rate Case. NYS DOL assigns this number when an agency requests the original wage schedule for a project. You can look up a previously requested schedule by its PRC number, and you need the PRC number to submit electronic certified payroll.
