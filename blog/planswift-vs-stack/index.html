PlanSwift vs STACK vs On-Screen Takeoff: which takeoff tool fits?

Prices and features checked October 17, 2026 (ET), on each vendor's own site. BuildWithBNZ publishes this page.

The short answer: In the PlanSwift vs STACK choice, the big split is cloud vs annual software plans. STACK runs in a browser. PlanSwift sells takeoff and estimating together on a yearly plan. On-Screen Takeoff (often called OST) is Windows desktop software that many estimators already know. All three now list AI tools that speed up takeoff. Pick the one that fits how your team works, then test it on your own plans.

First, two terms:

  • Takeoff means measuring the plans. You count fixtures, measure walls and figure square feet.
  • Estimate means putting prices on those quantities: labor, material and subs.

PlanSwift vs STACK: the main differences

1. Cloud vs plan-based software. STACK says it's "100% cloud-based," so you can work "from any device with an Internet connection" (STACK). PlanSwift's pricing page doesn't cover that. Check its system requirements page before you buy if your team uses Macs.

2. How you pay. PlanSwift lists two annual plans. Essential is $2,000 a year and Core is $3,000 a year, each for 1 seat. It says "Essential and Core are annual plans" (PlanSwift, Oct 17, 2026). STACK lists Premium at $299 and Pro at $349 per user per month, billed annually. It also shows a monthly billing option (STACK, Oct 17, 2026).

3. Free options. PlanSwift offers a 14-day free trial with no credit card. STACK has a free plan that includes 7 days of Pro. After that, it allows 1 active project a month, up to 300 plan pages and 50 measurements per project.

4. AI tools. PlanSwift Essential includes Auto Count, Auto Scale and Auto Bookmark. Core adds Auto Takeoff, which PlanSwift says "builds a first-pass takeoff for areas, lengths, and counts in minutes." STACK Premium lists AI Takeoffs, symbol search and STACK IQ. STACK says Premium includes "GPT-powered AI chat to quickly understand project details."

5. Estimating. PlanSwift puts takeoff and estimating in every plan, with pre-built assemblies (saved groups of items, like a wall type with its studs, drywall and screws). STACK puts worksheet-style estimating, assemblies and a regional cost library on its Pro plan.

Where On-Screen Takeoff fits

On-Screen Takeoff is from On Center Software. Its product page calls it "the unparalleled industry standard for takeoff software" (On Center, Oct 17, 2026). That's the vendor's own claim.

What the page shows:

  • It's Windows software. The system requirements list Windows 10 and older versions, and installation needs a local Windows administrator.
  • A free 14-day trial.
  • Tools like multi-condition takeoff, typical areas, and an overlay that shows what changed between two versions of a drawing.
  • A free one-plan Takeoff Boost preview that runs in your browser.

The product page doesn't list a price. Ask On Center for a quote.

PlanSwift vs OST

If you're choosing between PlanSwift and OST, the question is mostly about your team. OST will "seem natural" if you're a pro at manual takeoff, its page says. PlanSwift bundles estimating with assemblies in the same plan. Both offer a 14-day trial, so you can run the same job in each.

How to choose

  1. Pick one real job you've already bid. Run it in each trial.
  2. Include a hard sheet. Try a scanned page and your toughest trade.
  3. Time the whole thing, including fixes to the AI's first pass.
  4. Check where the numbers go. Does the estimate land where your team works?
  5. Read the terms. Ask about seats, renewals and cancellation before you sign.
