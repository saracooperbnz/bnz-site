# How to write a subcontractor invitation to bid

**The short answer:** A good invitation to bid (ITB) tells the right subs exactly what to price, by when, and in what format. Send it from a real person, to subs who fit the trade, area and job size. Attach or link the exact sheets and spec sections, the latest addendum and a bid form. Ask for a yes or no early, then follow up on a schedule until every sub has quoted, declined or given a reason.

## Who to invite

The best ITB in the world doesn't help if it goes to the wrong list.

- **Trade fit.** The sub does this scope, not "something close."
- **Area.** They work where the job is.
- **Size.** The package isn't too big or too small for them.
- **Track record.** They've quoted on time and their paperwork was clean on past jobs.
- **Requirements.** On public work: prevailing wage experience, bonding capacity, any required certifications.

Invite enough subs per trade that you still have comparable bids if some drop. Keep a note of who no-bids and why. That note is what makes your next list better.

## What to put in the invitation

**Subject line:** Project name, trade, due date. Example: *ITB: Main St Fit-Out, Electrical (Div 26), due Thu 10/29 2:00 PM ET*

**Body, in this order:**
1. **Project:** name, address, owner/architect, project type (public or private).
2. **Package:** the trade and a one-line scope description.
3. **Documents:** exact sheets and spec sections to price, plus Division 01, and the current addendum number. One link or attachment set.
4. **Scope sheet:** what's included, what's excluded, and what belongs to another trade.
5. **Bid form:** base bid, each alternate on its own line, unit prices, allowances, exclusions, and acknowledgment of every addendum by number.
6. **Due date and time,** with time zone, and where to send the bid.
7. **Questions:** one contact and an RFI cutoff date.
8. **Requirements:** insurance (COI and endorsements), bonding, prevailing wage, safety, schedule.
9. **Ask for intent:** "Please reply by [date] to say whether you'll bid."
10. **Signature:** a real person with a phone number.

## Sample ITB email

> **Subject:** ITB: [Project], [Trade] ([Division]), due [day date time zone]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> We're bidding [project] at [address] for [owner]. We'd like your price on the [trade] package.
>
> **Documents:** [link]. Please price sheets [list] and spec sections [list], plus Division 01. Current addendum: [#].
> **Scope sheet and bid form** are attached. Please use the bid form, list exclusions, and acknowledge each addendum by number.
> **Due:** [day, date, time, time zone], to this email.
> **Questions:** to me by [RFI cutoff].
> **Requirements:** [insurance / bond / prevailing wage / schedule].
>
> Can you reply by [date] to let me know if you'll bid?
>
> Thanks,
> [Name, title, company, phone]

## Follow-up schedule

Pick one and stick to it. A simple version:
- **After the intent date:** anyone who hasn't answered gets a short "are you bidding?" note.
- **Mid-way to due date:** check in with bidders. Send any addendum the same day it's issued, to everyone.
- **A few days before due:** confirm the time and format.
- **Day before:** last reminder to anyone still open.

Log every answer: quoting, declined, or a named reason ("too busy," "outside our area"). "Waiting" should always have a name attached.

## Send clarifications to everyone

If one sub asks a question that changes the scope, answer it in writing to every bidder in that trade. Otherwise you'll get bids priced on different scopes, and [leveling](/guides/bid-leveling/) gets harder.

## Common mistakes

- Sending the whole drawing set with no list of what to price.
- No bid form, so every proposal comes back in a different format.
- Due date with no time zone.
- Addenda sent only to subs who asked.
- Invitations from a no-reply portal address subs don't recognize.
- No follow-up, then wondering why only two bids came in.
- Forgetting insurance requirements until after award. See [COI tracking](/guides/subcontractor-coi-tracking/).

## FAQ

**What should a subcontractor invitation to bid include?**
Project, package and scope sheet, exact sheets and specs, current addendum, bid form, due date and time with time zone, a question contact, and requirements.

**How far ahead should I send invitations?**
As early as you have a complete package. Late invitations get fewer, rougher bids.

**How do I get more subs to respond?**
Invite the right fit, send from a person, make it easy to open, ask for intent early, and follow up on a schedule.
