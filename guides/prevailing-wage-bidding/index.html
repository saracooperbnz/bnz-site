Guide · Prevailing wage bidding (NY)

How to bid prevailing wage jobs in New York

On a New York public job, the wage schedule comes from the agency, not from you. The agency gets a prevailing rate schedule and a PRC number from the NYS DOL Bureau of Public Work and puts the schedule in the bid documents. On NYC public work, the NYC Comptroller's annual schedule applies. You price labor at those wage and supplement rates for every classification you'll use, for the whole project duration. You need to be registered with NYS DOL to bid. Once you're working, you submit certified payroll electronically at least every 30 days. We quote no rates here. Always use the official schedule.

Not legal advice. This is a general guide for contractors, not legal or payroll advice. Prevailing wage rules change, and the bid documents, the contracting agency and the official sources below control. Talk to a New York construction attorney or your payroll provider about your specific job.

1. Confirm the job is prevailing wage

Look for these in the bid documents:

  • A prevailing rate schedule bound into the specs, with a PRC number
  • References to NY Labor Law Article 8 (construction) or Article 9 (building service work)

NYS DOL requires the contracting agency to obtain the schedule, include it in the specifications, make it part of the contract, and file a notice of award (PW-16) (NYS DOL, Contracting Agency). Its FAQ says Article 8 covers public work and also some privately owned projects (Section 224-a) and certain renewable energy projects (Section 224-d) (NYS DOL, Electronic Payroll FAQ).

If the job looks covered and there's no schedule in the package, send a written RFI before you price it.

2. Get registered before you bid

NYS DOL says that as of December 30, 2024, all contractors and subcontractors submitting bids or doing construction work on Article 8 public work or covered private projects must register with the Department (NYS DOL, Bureau of Public Work). Your registration number is also needed for certified payroll. Make sure your subs are registered too. Ask for it in your invitation to bid.

Check the NYS DOL Contractor Registry page for current fees, renewal and any training requirements before you apply.

3. Find the right schedule and read it properly

  • Outside NYC: use the NYS DOL schedule issued for that project's PRC number. It's in the bid documents. NYS DOL also lets you view wage schedules on its site (NYS DOL, Bureau of Public Work).
  • NYC public work: the NYC Comptroller publishes the Construction Worker schedule and a separate Construction Apprentice schedule (NYC Comptroller, Wage Schedules). The current construction worker schedule was published 7/1/2026 for work July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027 (schedule PDF).

Three things estimators miss:

  1. Supplements are part of the rate. You owe wages and supplements (benefits) for each classification (NYS DOL PW39).
  2. Rates change mid-job. The Comptroller's schedule says contractors must pay the rates in effect when the work is performed. Preliminary schedules come out around June 1 and finals around July 1 each year (NYC Comptroller schedule). If your job crosses July 1, carry an escalation for the hours after it. Outside NYC, follow the update instructions on the NYS DOL schedule issued for your PRC number.
  3. Apprentice rates only apply to registered apprentices. Under Labor Law § 220(3-e), only apprentices individually registered in a NYS DOL-registered program your company participates in can be paid apprentice rates. Everyone else is paid as a journeyworker, and ratios apply (NYC Comptroller, Apprentice schedule).

4. Build the labor estimate from the schedule

This is the core of prevailing wage bid estimating:

  1. List every classification you'll use (for example painter, laborer, carpenter) and match it to the schedule's exact trade title. Classification is set by the work performed, not the worker's job title.
  2. Take the wage and supplement rate for each from the schedule. Don't use last job's numbers.
  3. Add overtime, shift and holiday rules as the schedule states them for that trade.
  4. Add your labor burden on top: payroll taxes, workers' comp and insurance. Ask your accountant or payroll provider how burden applies to supplements.
  5. Spread hours across the schedule periods if the job crosses July 1.
  6. Price subs the same way. Require subs to confirm they priced the project's schedule, then level sub bids with a row for "prevailing wage confirmed."

5. Watch addenda and the bid form

Public bids are strict. Acknowledge every addendum by number on the bid form. Addenda can change the scope, the bid date, or even the wage schedule. Keep a log of each addendum: date received, what changed, and who repriced. Good construction addendum tracking is what keeps a low number from becoming a rejected bid.

Bond requirements and Wicks Law (separate prime) thresholds vary by agency and project. Read them in the bid documents and the agency's procurement rules.

6. Line up insurance and paperwork before award

Agencies will ask for insurance certificates and endorsements fast after award, and so should you from your subs. Use our guide to track subcontractor insurance. For electronic certified payroll on public improvement projects, NYS DOL also asks for a copy of the payment bond (NYS DOL, Electronic Payroll).

7. Certified payroll once you start

From NYS DOL (Electronic Payroll; contractor user guide; FAQ), as of Oct 16, 2026:

  • Starting January 1, 2026, contractors and subcontractors on Article 8 projects submit certified payroll through the NYS DOL Certified Payroll portal, at least every 30 days for the length of the project. You need a NY.gov account.
  • You'll need: your FEIN, your NYS contractor registration number, the project PRC number, and, for public improvement projects, a copy of the payment bond.
  • Each employee record includes: name, address, Social Security number (if applicable) or birth date, hours and days worked, occupation, hourly rate, and supplements.
  • Penalties: the user guide says missing payrolls may draw $100/day after a 14-day grace period. The FAQ says DOL will announce publicly and email registered contractors before it starts issuing penalties for missing payrolls. Check the FAQ for whether enforcement has started.
  • NYC exception: NYC public work and NYC-permitted roadway excavation projects submit through NYC's own online certified payroll system instead.

Also post the schedule. The Comptroller's schedule says it must be posted in a prominent place on site, and covered workers must get the schedule and Worker Notice when the project begins (NYC Comptroller schedule).

One change on hold

NYS DOL says amendments to Labor Law § 220(3)(f) (custom fabrication) that were scheduled to take effect June 18, 2026 are suspended by federal court order until further notice, and DOL isn't enforcing them while the order stands (NYS DOL, Enforcement Guidance, Oct 16, 2026). Check again before you bid work with off-site fabrication.

Common mistakes

  • Pricing from an old schedule, or the wrong county's schedule.
  • Leaving out supplements.
  • Paying apprentice rates to unregistered apprentices.
  • No escalation for work after July 1.
  • Subs who aren't registered, or who priced open-shop rates.
  • Missing an addendum acknowledgment on the bid form.

FAQ

Asked on every call.

Where do I find the prevailing wage rates for a New York public job? +
Use the schedule in the bid documents. The contracting agency must obtain a prevailing rate schedule from the NYS Department of Labor's Bureau of Public Work and include it in the contract specifications. For New York City public work, the NYC Comptroller publishes the construction worker schedule each year, effective July 1.
Do I have to register with NYS DOL to bid public work? +
According to NYS DOL, as of December 30, 2024, contractors and subcontractors submitting bids or performing construction work on public work projects or private projects covered by Article 8 of the Labor Law must register with the Department.
How often do I submit certified payroll in New York? +
NYS DOL says that starting January 1, 2026, contractors and subcontractors on Article 8 projects must submit certified payroll electronically through its Certified Payroll portal at least every 30 days. NYC public work uses NYC's own online certified payroll system.
What is a PRC number? +
A Prevailing Rate Case number is NYS DOL's tracking number for a prevailing wage project. It's issued with the wage schedule, and contractors need it to submit electronic certified payroll.
