# Estimating software for electrical contractors: what to look for in 2026

*Checked October 20, 2026 (ET). Prices come from each vendor's own site on that date. BuildWithBNZ publishes this page.*

**The short answer:** Good estimating software for electrical contractors does three things well. It counts devices and fixtures from the plans. It turns those counts into labor and material with your own rates. And it makes changes fast when an addendum drops. Some tools are built only for electrical work. Others are general takeoff tools you set up for electrical. Test one or two on a job you've already bid before you sign anything.

A few terms first:

- **Takeoff** means measuring the plans. In electrical work, that's counting receptacles, switches and fixtures, and measuring conduit and wire runs.
- A **labor unit** is the time it takes to install one item, like one receptacle or one foot of conduit.
- An **assembly** is a saved group of parts that go together, like a receptacle with its box, cover plate and wire.
- A **fixture schedule** is the table on the drawings that lists each light fixture type.
- **Addenda** are changes to the plans or specs issued after the bid package goes out.

## What estimating software for electrical contractors should do

Use this as your checklist in every demo.

**1. Count from the plans.** Can it count devices and fixtures on a PDF? Does it use AI to find symbols, or do you click each one?

**2. Measure runs.** Can it measure conduit and wire, including drops and bends? Can you add a waste factor?

**3. Use your labor units.** Does it come with labor units? Can you change them to match your crew's real speed?

**4. Use your prices.** Can you load your supplier's prices, or are you stuck with a national list?

**5. Handle assemblies.** Can you build your own assemblies, so one click adds every part?

**6. Handle changes.** When an addendum adds ten fixtures, how fast can you update the bid?

**7. Produce a clean proposal.** Can you send a proposal to the GC or owner straight from the tool?

**8. Fit your size.** Is the price per user, per company or per estimate? A one-estimator shop and a ten-estimator shop pay very differently.

## How to estimate electrical work, step by step

Whatever tool you use, the steps are about the same.

1. **Read the whole set.** Go through the electrical sheets, the specs and every addendum. Check the fixture schedule and the panel schedule (the table showing each panel's circuits).
2. **Count devices and fixtures.** Count each receptacle, switch and light fixture by type.
3. **Measure runs.** Measure conduit and wire from panel to devices. Add a waste factor.
4. **Apply labor units.** Multiply each count by its labor unit to get hours.
5. **Price labor.** Multiply hours by your labor rate. On public work, use the prevailing wage (the set pay rate the government requires) from the job's wage schedule.
6. **Price material.** Use current quotes from your supplier, not last year's prices.
7. **Add the rest.** Include equipment, permits, overhead and profit.
8. **List what's missing.** Write down anything not in the set. Ask before bid day, or state it as an exclusion or assumption.

Software speeds up steps 2 through 6. It doesn't replace step 8.

## Electrical-only tools

These are built just for electrical contractors.

**Amperio.ai.** Its site calls it "estimating software designed specifically for electrical installations." You can estimate "by outlet, circuit, panel, or conduit foot." Plans listed on its pricing page ([Amperio.ai](https://amperio.ai/en/pricing), Oct 20, 2026):
- PRO, "for independent electrical contractors": $49 a month, with 10 estimates a month.
- BUSINESS: $99 a month plus $15 per additional user, with 100 estimates a month.
- GLOBAL: $179 a month, with unlimited estimates and users.

It says you can start free and that estimates export to Excel and PDF. AI use is sold as separate credits on the PRO and BUSINESS plans.

**ConEst IntelliBid.** ConEst offers IntelliBid for electrical estimating and SureCount for digital takeoff ([ConEst](https://www.conest.com/)). Its site asks you to request a demo. We found no published price.

**Trimble Accubid Anywhere.** Trimble's MEP site lists Accubid Anywhere as "estimating software for electrical & ICT contractors." (ICT means data and communications wiring.) It says Trimble's estimating and takeoff tools feature "AI-powered takeoff and up-to-date material pricing" ([Trimble MEP](https://www.trimble.com/en/industries/construction/mep)). We found no published price. Ask for a quote.

## Software or electrical estimating services?

Some electrical shops don't buy software at all. They send plans to an outside **electrical estimating service** that does the takeoff and pricing for them.

A service can make sense when:
- You bid more jobs than you can price yourself.
- You're bidding a type of work you don't usually do.

Software makes more sense when:
- You have someone with time to run it.
- You want to see and control every count.
- Addenda come late and you can't wait on someone else.

Either way, you still review the number. You sign the bid. For the same choice across all trades, see [takeoff services vs AI takeoff software](/blog/construction-takeoff-services/).

## How to test estimating software on your own bid

1. **Pick a job you already bid.** You know the right answer.
2. **Include a hard sheet.** Try a scanned page or a crowded lighting plan.
3. **Count one room by hand.** Compare it to the software's count.
4. **Load your own labor units and prices.** Don't judge it on default numbers.
5. **Run an addendum.** Add or remove fixtures and time the update.
6. **Ask about the term.** Monthly, yearly or one-time? What happens if you cancel?
