# How to track subcontractor insurance: a COI guide for GCs

**The short answer:** Collect a certificate of insurance (COI) from every sub before they set foot on site. Check it against what your subcontract requires: coverage types, limits, dates, certificate holder and additional insured status. Ask for the endorsements, not just the certificate. Then put every expiration date somewhere that will remind you before it lapses, and request the renewal ahead of time. A COI you collected once and filed is not tracking.

*This guide is general information for US contractors, not legal or insurance advice. Your subcontract, your owner contract and your broker set the actual requirements.*

## Why GCs track sub insurance at all

If an uninsured or underinsured sub causes damage or someone gets hurt, the claim can land on you. Owner contracts often require you to flow insurance requirements down to every sub. Tracking COIs is how you prove you did.

## What a COI is, and what it isn't

In the US, most COIs come on the ACORD 25 form, issued by the sub's agent or broker. It lists policies, insurers, policy numbers, effective and expiration dates, limits and the certificate holder.

What it isn't: the policy. The form itself says it's for information only and doesn't amend or extend coverage. That's why the endorsements matter.

## What to check on every certificate

Go down the certificate against your subcontract's insurance exhibit:

1. **Named insured.** Matches the legal name on the subcontract and W-9.
2. **Coverage types.** Usually general liability, auto, workers' comp and employer's liability, and umbrella/excess. Some trades need more (professional, pollution). Your contract decides.
3. **Limits.** Each one meets or beats what the subcontract requires.
4. **Dates.** Effective now and not expiring before the work is done, or tracked for renewal.
5. **Certificate holder.** Your company, correct legal name and address.
6. **Additional insured.** Box checked, and the actual endorsement attached, naming who your contract requires (often you, the owner and others).
7. **Waiver of subrogation and primary/non-contributory wording,** if your contract requires them, backed by endorsements.
8. **Description of operations.** References the project if your contract asks for it.
9. **Producer contact.** So you know who to call to verify or request a renewal.

If anything doesn't match, send it back in writing before the sub mobilizes.

## Set up a simple COI tracker

You don't need special software to start. One sheet per company (not per project) works, with a row per sub and these columns:

| Column | Why |
|---|---|
| Sub legal name | Matches subcontract and W-9 |
| Trade | Filters by package |
| Active projects | Which jobs they're on |
| GL / Auto / WC / Umbrella expiration | One column each, since they renew on different dates |
| Limits OK? (Y/N) | Checked against the contract |
| Additional insured endorsement received? | The certificate isn't enough |
| W-9 on file? | Collected at the same time |
| Broker contact | Who to email for renewals |
| Last checked / by whom | Accountability |
| Status | Compliant / Expiring / Expired / Missing |

The column that matters most is the earliest expiration date. Sort by it.

## Put expirations on a calendar

A tracker only works if someone looks at it. Put each expiration on a shared calendar with a reminder far enough ahead to request the renewal from the broker. Pick a lead time your team can actually work with and stick to it.

## The routine that keeps it current

- **At bid or invitation:** tell subs your insurance requirements up front. See [how to write a sub bid invitation](/guides/subcontractor-bid-invitations/).
- **Before the subcontract is signed:** COI, endorsements and W-9 in hand and checked.
- **Before mobilization:** confirm nothing has expired since.
- **Ahead of each expiration:** email the sub and their broker for the renewed certificate.
- **On expiration with no renewal:** decide with your PM whether the sub can stay on site. Write the decision down.
- **At closeout:** file the final certificates with the project records.

## Common mistakes

- Accepting a COI without the additional insured endorsement.
- Tracking one expiration date per sub when each policy renews separately.
- Letting the sub email certificates to whoever they know at your company, so they never reach the tracker.
- Checking at the start of a job and never again.

## FAQ

**What is a certificate of insurance in construction?**
A summary from the sub's broker showing policies, limits, dates and the certificate holder, usually on an ACORD 25. It's evidence of coverage, not the policy.

**Does a COI prove I'm an additional insured?**
Not by itself. Get the endorsement and check it with your broker.

**When should a GC collect a sub's COI?**
Before the sub starts work, and again at every renewal.

**What's the most common COI tracking mistake?**
Collecting it once and never looking at the dates again.
