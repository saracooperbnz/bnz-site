Free AI construction estimating software: what you actually get

Checked October 16, 2026 (ET). BuildWithBNZ publishes this page. It's priced per project, and we say so below. Every competitor fact links to its source.

The short answer: If you search for AI construction estimating software free of charge, you'll mostly find free trials, not free plans. The one free plan we found (STACK) has usage limits. And free options don't always include the AI features. So "free" usually means "free to test," not "free to bid with."

Two terms before we start:

  • Takeoff means measuring the plans: counting doors, measuring walls, figuring square feet.
  • Estimate means putting prices on those quantities: labor, material and subs.

Most AI tools speed up takeoff. Fewer price the estimate. Free options usually give you a small slice of either one.

STACK: a free plan with limits

STACK is the only free plan on our list. It caps active projects, plan pages and measurements. See STACK's pricing page for the current limits. Caps like these can go fast on a real job. A small remodel might fit. A commercial bid likely won't. Check which AI features, if any, the free plan includes before you count on them.

Kreo: try AI takeoff before you buy

Kreo lists a free trial. Its cheapest plan, Essential, has no AI. AI takeoff, auto symbol counting and its AI assistant, Caddie, start on the Pro plan (Kreo, Oct 16, 2026). Ask whether the trial includes Pro's AI features. For prices and terms, see Kreo's pricing page.

Handoff: a trial for residential builders

Handoff is AI estimating for residential builders and remodelers. It lists a free trial on its Flex plan. AI takeoffs from residential plans are listed on its higher Scale plan (Handoff, Oct 16, 2026). For prices and contract terms, see Handoff's pricing page.

Togal.AI: AI takeoff, paid

Togal.AI's Growth plan is $299 a month per user, billed yearly (Togal, Oct 16, 2026). Check its site for current trial or demo options.

What free AI construction estimating software leaves out

A free plan or trial is a good way to test a tool. But it's rarely enough to run your bids. Here's what tends to be missing:

  1. Volume. Caps on projects or measurements stop you on a busy week.
  2. The AI itself. On some tools, the AI features sit on paid plans only. Kreo is one example.
  3. Your own prices. Takeoff isn't an estimate. You still need your labor rates and supplier prices.
  4. The sub side. None of the free options we found send bid invites, chase sub quotes or collect insurance certificates (COIs).
  5. Time. Trials are short. Plan your test before you start the clock.

How to test a free trial the right way

  • Pick one real bid. Use a job you've already priced, so you can check the AI's numbers against yours.
  • Include a hard sheet. Try a scanned drawing and your hardest trade, not just a clean floor plan.
  • Time it. Track how long the takeoff takes, including fixes.
  • Check the export. Can you get the quantities into the tool or spreadsheet your team already uses?
  • Read the renewal terms. Most paid plans above are billed annually.
